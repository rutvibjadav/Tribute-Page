01 / The journey

Biography

From a coastal town in Tamil Nadu to the highest constitutional office in India.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Curious about the world from an early age, he studied physics before pursuing aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology, completing the course in 1960.

He went on to serve India’s scientific community at the Defence Research and Development Organisation and the Indian Space Research Organisation. At ISRO, he led the project that developed the SLV-III, which placed the Rohini satellite into orbit in 1980. He later became a central figure in the country’s guided missile development programme.

In 2002, Kalam became the 11th President of India, serving until 2007. After his presidency, he returned to teaching and writing, meeting students across the country and encouraging them to pursue knowledge and public service. His books include Wings of Fire and Ignited Minds.

On 27 July 2015, while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, Kalam collapsed and died. He was 83. His final hours reflected the work he had chosen throughout his life: sharing ideas with students.

04 / The work continues

Legacy and Inspiration

Dr. Kalam brought scientific ambition into public life and made learning feel like a shared national purpose. His work in space research and missile development helped build India's technical capacity; his years in the classroom showed where he believed the future belonged.

His example continues to encourage students, educators, and citizens to pair curiosity with discipline and service. The most enduring tribute is to carry that spirit of learning forward.
